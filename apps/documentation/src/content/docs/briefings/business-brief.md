## The model

Free now, paid tiers later. The desktop app itself (recording, screenshots, the
quick editor) stays free with no account required. The planned monetization is the
**cloud capability**: a paid cloud tier for storing and sharing recordings.
Critically, paying is optional even once cloud exists — if you don't want Kaipu's
cloud, you can already get your file out and share it through your own Google
Drive or cloud account. Kaipu is only paid for _if you want Kaipu to host and hand
out the shareable link for you_.

## Who it's for first

The beachhead is **any indie creator** — that's the primary "just use it" audience.
Alongside that, the founder sees a second real use case from direct experience:
small teams sharing internal videos as a lighter alternative to Loom (the founder
cited wanting exactly this at MGM Resorts — reaching for Loom to share videos
internally, and thinking a lightweight desktop app like Kaipu could serve that need
instead). This is casual team video-sharing, not a collaboration platform — see
Non-goals.

## How people find it

Primarily word of mouth and the landing page (kaipu.app) — organic discovery is the
main bet. Content/SEO/social is a secondary channel, not the primary one.

## What success looks like

**Paid cloud conversions** — people upgrading to the paid cloud tier is the signal
that matters. No specific number or percentage has been committed to yet; treat
"conversions to paid cloud exist and are growing" as the signal, not a fixed target.

## What it will never be

- **An enterprise or team-collaboration platform.** No org admin, no SSO, no
  seat-based team plans — even the team use case above is "share a video with
  people," not workspace/collaboration tooling.
- **A cloud-first product.** Per the product's own philosophy: the recording is the
  product, cloud is a capability you opt into, never the thing being sold as the
  core experience.
- **A general or advanced video editor.** Not a Final Cut or Premiere competitor —
  the founder's own framing: "quick and smooth," not an advanced editing tool.
- **A cloud lock-in play.** Local-first isn't just a technical choice — the founder
  wants the app to stay useful even for someone who never pays: if you don't need
  Kaipu's cloud, your own Google Drive or cloud account is a fine substitute for
  sharing. The paid tier is a convenience Kaipu offers, not a dependency it creates.
