## Context

The video editor's main track is one ordered list, `scene.items`, of clips of a single recording
and full-frame image slides, laid end to end with no gap
(`features/video-editor/scene.ts`, `timeline.ts`). Zooms, privacy regions, muted spans, the
webcam layout and the cursor are stored in seconds of the recording, so they follow the footage
through every trim, split and reorder with no code. Annotations are stored in timeline seconds.

[PR #620](https://github.com/niway-dev/kaipu-record/pull/620) gave each frame capture an **Add to
timeline** button that inserts the capture as a 3 s slide in that list. The owner tested it on
2026-11-13 and asked for a different model, with a sketch: the Clips section holds objects on
several levels; an added image or video can sit before the recording, after it, or above it;
items that fit on one level share it; overlapping items are both on screen; items take effects
such as a fade into the next clip.

ADR 0040 (2026-10-31, parked 2026-11-06, nothing built) had designed added media as separate
"Overlay" lanes above Clips, anchored to timeline time, with transitions out of scope. It names
the condition that would move it to footage-anchored items: users reporting that cuts misalign
overlays from what they illustrate. Joining media in the sequence makes that misalignment the
normal case rather than a report: every item inserted before the recording shifts the recording
under every timeline-anchored item.

## Decision

1. **One section.** Added media lives inside Clips. There are no separate Overlay lanes.
2. **The bottom level is the sequence.** It stays what `scene.items` is today: items end to end,
   no gaps, and an item inserted there pushes everything after it later. Joining a photo or a
   video before or after the recording is an insert in this list. The recording is never placed
   at a free position; it moves only because something before it pushes it.
3. **Upper levels float.** A floating item never pushes anything. It has a duration in timeline
   seconds, a box in the output frame (normalized centre and width, aspect locked, inside the
   picture like the webcam layer) and a stacking order `z`. Two floating items that overlap in
   time are both drawn, higher `z` in front; an item that covers the whole frame hides what is
   under it. A new floating image starts fitted to the whole frame.
4. **Anchored to the footage under its start.** A floating item stores where it starts as a
   position in the footage, not in the timeline: a second of the recording when it starts over a
   clip, or an offset into a sequence item when it starts over a slide. Its timeline start comes
   from the clip whose half-open source range `[sourceStart, sourceEnd)` holds the anchor second,
   so at a split point it lands at the start of the later clip. `sourceToTimeline` cannot be used
   as is: it accepts a clip's end and returns the first match. The item plays continuously from
   there across later cuts. An item whose anchor is in deleted footage is not placed, not drawn
   and not exported, and comes back when the footage does. Zoom segments also come back with
   their footage, but unlike them a cut under the start hides the whole item, even when the rest
   of the footage it covered survives.
5. **Rows are computed, stacking is stored.** The level an item is drawn on in the timeline is
   derived on every layout: `row = 1 + the highest row among the lower-z items it overlaps in
time`, or the first upper row when it overlaps none. An item goes to a higher row
   only when it overlaps one below it, so items that overlap nothing share the first upper row;
   an empty row does not exist. `z` is the only stored order, so an edit elsewhere can move an
   item to another row but never changes which of two overlapping items is in front.
6. **Effects.** A floating item fades in and out (opacity). Between two sequence items the first
   transition is a fade through the background, which needs one decoded source at a time. A true
   crossfade overlaps the two items and shortens the video by its length; it ships with floating
   videos, which bring the second decoder.
7. **Durations.** An image defaults to 10 s floating and 5 s in the sequence. A video keeps its
   own length and can be trimmed.
8. **Sound.** A floating or joined video's sound is on by default with a mute per item and no
   volume control, mixed at export as 0040 describes.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Every level free, nothing pushes** (the Premiere and Resolve track model). One rule for every
  item. Rejected: joining means moving the recording by hand, an accidental drag leaves seconds
  of empty video in the export, and every gap needs its own rule for sound and picture.
- **Separate Overlay lanes above Clips** (ADR 0040). Rejected by the owner's sketch: the items
  are the same kind of object as a clip and belong in the same section.
- **Timeline-anchored floating items** (ADR 0040). Rejected: with joins, any insert before the
  recording leaves every floating item over a different moment.
- **Anchor as sequence item id plus offset for clips.** Rejected: trimming the head of the clip
  changes what the same offset points at. A second of the recording does not move.
- **The row as the stacking order.** Rejected: automatic packing would then change which image
  is in front when an unrelated item is shortened.
- **Gaps in the sequence.** Rejected for now by the owner ("sin huecos por ahora").

## Consequences

- `scene.media` is a new optional list; a scene without it opens as today.
- Stage 2 (a second video in the sequence) gives clips and source anchors a source id. Until
  then "the recording" is the only source and the anchor carries only a time. Superseded by
  [0058](/architecture/decisions/0058-joined-recordings-share-one-source-axis-each-on-its-own-range/):
  each joined recording gets its own range on one source axis, so the anchor stays a time.
- Annotations still use timeline seconds and now misalign on every insert before the recording,
  not only on a mid-timeline delete. Moving them to the same anchor is a follow-up, listed in the
  spec; it needs a migration of saved scenes.
- A cut that removes the second a floating item starts on hides the whole item, even when the
  rest of the footage it covered survives. Approved by the owner on 2026-11-14 for the first
  stage, and the spec's question 4 revisits it once stage 1 runs in the real app; the alternative is to move its start to the next surviving second.
- A floating item longer than what remains of the video is cut at the video's end; it never
  extends the video.
- The owner's sketch 3 (an item on the upper level from 0, the recording starting later and
  overlapping its end) is not representable as drawn. It reads either as the crossfade of
  decision 6 or as empty background before the recording; the owner approved the crossfade on
  2026-11-14.
- Preview and export must compute floating items from one pure function of the scene, the layout
  and the time, or they will disagree.

## Non-goals

- Masks, rotation, crop or blend modes on floating items.
- Zooms, privacy regions or the cursor on added videos.
- Volume, ducking or audio fades other than the crossfade's.
- Free lanes for zooms, privacy, annotations or the camera (0040 stands).

## What would reopen this

- The owner wants empty time before or between items: add an explicit background block as a
  sequence item, with a duration. Not free positioning.
- Users want an item to stay at an output time across cuts (a title over the first seconds, music):
  add a per-item "pin to the timeline" anchor kind.
- Sketch 3 is read as empty background: the block above.
- Measurements show the preview holds more than two simultaneous videos, or not two: move the
  limit (0040).
