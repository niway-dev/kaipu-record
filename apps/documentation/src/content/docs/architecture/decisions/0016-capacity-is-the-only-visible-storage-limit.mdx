# ADR 0016: Capacity is the only visible storage limit — no credits, invisible fair-use, read-only over quota, no deletion

## Context

On 2026-10-08 the owner set the storage policy: no credits, and the only limit a user ever
sees is `capacity − used`. Its consequences were not written down and partly contradict
existing documents:

- The trial spec (2026-09-15) says over-quota content may be deleted 60 days after notice,
  and the published Cloud Terms copy (`legal.cloud` in `packages/i18n/messages/*.json`) says
  the same.
- `cloud-limits.ts` counts a persisted thumbnail toward capacity, and
  `cloud_revision.thumbnail_bytes` feeds accounting.
- Sharing ([ADR 0013](./0013-sharing-stable-link-plus-access-grants)) needs a cost guard that
  is not a user-visible quota.

## Decision

1. **The only visible limit is capacity.** Users see `capacity − used`. No credits, no
   bandwidth or view quota in the UI.
2. **Invisible fair-use on shared playback.** Per share 1,000 views/day; per owner 5,000/day
   (free) and 25,000/day (pro); a view is one successful non-owner resolve. Over a cap the
   watch page shows "temporarily unavailable, try later" to non-owners until 00:00 UTC; the
   owner still plays; at most one owner email per day; an operator can lift the cap per
   account in the Console. Caps are server configuration (`PROPOSAL`), never shown as a quota.
3. **Over quota → read-only, no deletion.** After a downgrade or a capacity reduction, new
   uploads are blocked; reads, downloads, shares (existing and new) and deletes keep working.
   Kaipu never deletes a user's files because of quota.
4. **Derived files are free.** Thumbnails, posters, GIFs and presets are excluded from quota.
5. **Inactive free accounts are archived, not deleted.** After 12 months without sign-in or
   upload: shares paused (watch page "unavailable"), uploads blocked, files kept. Two warning
   emails, 30 and 7 days before. Signing in restores everything.

## Consequences

**Positive:**

- One number to explain to users; no surprise loss of files.
- Sharing has a cost guard that never appears as a product limit.

**Negative:**

- Storage of over-quota and archived accounts is paid for indefinitely; archiving bounds the
  serving cost, not the storage cost.
- Implementation work (separate sub-issue): drop derived bytes from `reconcile()` and the
  quota SQL (only ever grows free space); over-quota read-only enforcement; the archive job
  and emails; and the **Cloud Terms copy must change** (legal review) — until it does, the
  published Terms still describe the 60-day rule.

**Neutral:**

- The trial spec's other reduced-quota rules (restrict new uploads, keep download/delete,
  global Cloud flag) are unchanged and consistent with this ADR.

## Alternatives considered

### Keep the 60-day deletion rule

Rejected by the owner on 2026-10-08: deleting user files to recover quota is the failure the
cloud product promise exists to avoid.

### A visible bandwidth / view quota

Rejected: a second number to explain, and it punishes the owner for a link going viral.

### Credits

Rejected on 2026-10-08.

## When to revisit

- If the storage held by archived or over-quota accounts becomes a material cost.
- If fair-use caps trigger for legitimate use often enough that they read as a quota.
